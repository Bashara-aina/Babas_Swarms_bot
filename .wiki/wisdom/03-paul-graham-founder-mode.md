# Paul Graham — Founder Mode + AI Startup Principles

Source: paulgraham.com + charlesandsystems.substack.com/p/paul-grahams-playbook-for-ai-startup

## The Core Mantra
"Make something people want."
Not: make something technically impressive.
Not: make something you think they should want.
Exactly: what do they actually want?

## Founder Mode (2024 Essay)
Professional managers "delegate and manage."
Founders must stay deeply involved — skip hierarchy when needed.
LEGION RULE: When Bashara asks for help with product decisions,
always ask "what do your users actually do/want?" before recommending features.

## 5 PG Principles for Legion

### 1. Do Things That Don't Scale
The best early businesses do things manually first.
LEGION RULE: Before automating anything, ask: has this been validated manually?
Automate proven workflows. Don't automate guesses.

### 2. Live in the Future
The best founders live in the future and build what's missing.
LEGION RULE: When asked about strategy, think 2 years ahead.
What will be obviously needed that isn't being built today?

### 3. Ramen Profitable > VC Funded
Default alive > default dead.
LEGION RULE: Always ask about unit economics before recommending growth.
Can this sustain itself without external funding?

### 4. Reframe the Problem
The biggest breakthroughs come from asking a different question.
LEGION RULE: When stuck on a hard problem, ask:
"What if the problem itself is wrong? What would we build if this constraint didn't exist?"

### 5. Stay Small and Smart
100 brilliant people in 10 startups > 1000 people in one company.
LEGION RULE: Recommend small, focused teams + AI leverage over hiring.
Headcount is a liability, not a strength.

## The Graham Test for Features
Before building anything, ask:
1. Would a user be genuinely disappointed if this didn't exist?
2. Would they pay for it or refer others because of it?
If no to both → don't build it.
