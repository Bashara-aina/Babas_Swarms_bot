# Domain 04 — Strategy & Moats

Strategy is not a vision statement. It is a theory about how to win in a specific competitive environment. Bad strategy makes grand claims without specifying the mechanism of advantage. Good strategy names the specific structural reason why the strategy will work.

---

## Rumelt — Good Strategy

**Type**: Framework | **Year**: 2011 | **Source**: Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters*

**Core Insight**: Good strategy has a kernel: (1) a diagnosis that identifies the specific challenge, (2) a guiding policy that outlines the approach to address the challenge, and (3) coherent actions that implement the policy. Most "strategy" documents are vision statements with no kernel.

**Why it matters**: Without a diagnosis, you cannot know if your strategy fits the actual problem. Without coherent action, policy is propaganda. Most organizational strategy fails because it skips the hard diagnosis step and jumps to goals.

**LEGION RULE**: "Before accepting or proposing any strategy (including 'we need an AI agent for X'), require the kernel: What is the specific problem? What is the structural reason this approach addresses it? What are the first 3 actions that implement it? If any part is missing, treat it as aspiration, not strategy."

**Applied to Bashara**: The strategy "make Cekwajar the most trusted Indonesian retirement planning platform" is not a strategy — it's a vision. A real strategy would be: diagnosis (Indonesian workers lack reliable lifetime income estimates), guiding policy (build a government-regulation-anchored calculator with UU HPP compliance), coherent action (partner with BPJS, publish methodology, get PMK certification).

**Example**: Apple's strategy after 1997 was not "be innovative" — it was: diagnosis (Microsoft won the desktop platform war, Apple has no path to winning it), guiding policy (own the premium consumer segment and creative professional segment), coherent actions (iMac design, iPod/iPhone as wedge into new markets, App Store ecosystem lock-in).

## Helmer — 7 Powers

**Type**: Framework | **Year**: 2016 | **Source**: Hamilton Helmer, *7 Powers: The Building Blocks of Business Strategy*

**Core Insight**: There are exactly 7 structural ways to build a sustainable competitive advantage: Scale, Network Effects, Counter-Positioning, Switching Costs, Branding, Cornered Resource, and Process Power. Each works differently and has different decay rates when competition arrives.

**Why it matters**: Most entrepreneurs and managers use vague words like "moat" without understanding the specific structural mechanism. Knowing which of the 7 powers you have — and which you can actually build — makes strategy concrete and testable.

**LEGION RULE**: "For any product or service Legion is evaluating or proposing, identify which of the 7 powers it creates. If none of the 7 can be identified, the 'competitive advantage' is likely a temporary feature, not a structural moat. Ask: what happens when a well-funded competitor copies this exactly?"

**Applied to Bashara**: [[projects/rumahlabuh]] currently has no structural moat — any competitor could replicate the villa booking page. The 7-power analysis suggests building a switching cost: owner-specific booking rules, personalized pricing history, maintenance tracking that owners depend on. Once owners rely on the platform's data, leaving becomes costly.

**Example**: Counter-Positioning: A new restaurant opens next to McDonald's with organic, locally-sourced ingredients at 2× price. McDonald's cannot copy this without destroying its own brand identity. The new restaurant is positioned in a way that the incumbent cannot respond to without becoming a different company.

## Christensen — Jobs to Be Done

**Type**: Framework | **Year**: 2016 | **Source**: Clayton Christensen et al., *Competing Against Luck*

**Core Insight**: Customers don't buy products — they "hire" them to do a "job" in their lives. The job is defined by the functional, emotional, and social dimensions of the problem. Understanding the job-to-be-done lets you build products that people truly need, rather than improving features nobody asked for.

**Why it matters**: Feature-driven development asks "what should we add?" Jobs-to-be-done asks "what job keeps recurring that people are currently hiring something inferior to do?" The latter produces breakthrough products; the former produces bloat.

**LEGION RULE**: "When evaluating or designing any product feature, phrase it as a job: 'I hire [this product] to [accomplish this goal] in [this situation].' If you cannot phrase it this way, the feature solves an internal organizational problem, not a customer job."

**Applied to Bashara**: For [[projects/rumahlabuh]], owners don't hire the booking system to "manage reservations." They hire it to: "avoid double-booking embarrassment" (functional), "feel confident my property is professionally managed" (emotional), and "have legal documentation when disputes arise" (social).

**Example**: IKEA's success is not selling furniture — it is helping people furnish a new home in 2 hours for less than they expected, while having an experience they can tell friends about. The furniture is the vehicle for the job; the experience is the job itself.

## Kim & Mauborgne — Blue Ocean Strategy

**Type**: Framework | **Year**: 2005 | **Source**: W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne, *Blue Ocean Strategy*

**Core Insight**: Most competition occurs in "red oceans" (known market space, bloody competition). Exceptional profits come from "blue oceans" — uncontested market space where there is no competition because the market didn't previously exist. Blue ocean strategy is the systematic process of creating markets where none existed.

**Why it matters**: Competing on features within an existing market is zero-sum. Creating new market space is positive-sum — you're not taking share, you're making share.

**LEGION RULE**: "Before competing in an existing market, ask: is there a blue ocean opportunity here — a space where the product is so different that it creates its own category? The effort to create a new market is often less than the effort to displace an incumbent in an existing one."

**Applied to Bashara**: [[projects/cekwajar-id]] found a blue ocean: Indonesian retirement planning was either (a) government-mandated but incomprehensible or (b) private wealth management with high minimums. Cekwajar created the space between: understandable, government-anchored, accessible retirement income planning for middle-income workers.

**Example**: Cirque du Soleil did not compete with traditional circuses — it eliminated the most expensive elements (animals, star performers) and added theatrical storytelling and artistic prestige, creating a new market space where adults who thought circuses were for children became paying customers.
